# Chili con carne (Texas red / bowl of red)

**Prep Time:** 45 minutes  
**Cook Time:** 1-1.5 hours  
**Servings:** 6 persons

## Ingredients

### Base (same for both versions)

- 1 kg of ground beef (other options also fine, but ground beef is just super convenient)
- 1 tbsp oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 4-5 garlic cloves
- 2-3 tsp ground cumin
- 1-2 tsp oregano
- 1 tsp ground coriander (optional)
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1-1.5 tsp salt to start, then adjust
- 750 ml beef broth, adding more if needed
- thickening agent of your choice, e.g. a scoop of cornstarch (maizena) slurry
- 1 can/bottle of Guinness (or whatever other dark beer) to deglaze the pan after browning the meat (optional)
- 1 tbsp cocoa powder
- 2-3 dried porcini, ground fine (optional)
- a splash of vinegar + 1 tbsp of (brown) sugar

### Version A: whole dried chiles

- 4 ancho (~55 g)
- 3 guajillo (~25 g)
- 2-3 pasilla (~15 g)
- Optional heat: 1-2 dried chipotles, or 1-2 chiles de árbol

### Version B: no whole chiles

- 5-6 tbsp sweet paprika
- 1.5-2 tbsp smoked paprika
- 1-3 chipotles in adobo, minced, plus 1-2 tbsp of the sauce

## Instructions

1. Version A only: toast the chiles (in a dry pan), de-seed, soak and blend them with a little water into a paste. Consistency should be like cream, ideally no chile chunks remaining.
2. Brown the beef: while the chiles soak, heat the oil in a pot over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef (use multiple batches depending on your pot size), breaking it into coarse chunks rather than fine crumbles. Let it sit undisturbed long enough to get real browning. Set the browned beef aside.
3. Soften the onion: lower the heat, add the onion and cook, scraping up the browned bits, until soft and lightly golden (5-ish mins).
4. Bloom garlic and spices: add the garlic, cumin, oregano, coriander (optional) and black pepper. Stir constantly for about 1 minute until fragrant. Version B: add the sweet and smoked paprika here too and bloom it in the fat for under a minute on low heat, because it burns easily.
5. Deglaze with the beer (or a splash of broth if you skip the beer), scrape up the browned bits in the pot.
6. Return the beef, then add the chile paste (Version A) or the chipotles and adobo sauce (Version B). Add the ground porcini (optional), the cocoa powder, sugar and vinegar.
7. Add the beef broth and the starting salt: add as much broth as needed, the meat should be submerged, but it should not be too liquid.
8. Simmer for 1-1.5 hours on low heat, stirring now and then. Add a splash of broth if it gets too thick before the flavors have melded.
9. Thicken with the cornstarch slurry right before serving and bring to a boil once more.
10. Taste and adjust the salt.

## Serving

Serve over rice, sides could be e.g. some grilled fajita vegetables.

Garnishes, if you're not a purist: chopped raw onion, shredded cheddar, lime wedges, pickled jalapeños, Doritos.

## Trouble Shoot

- Tastes harsh and aggressive right after adding the broth: normal, this mellows out during the simmer.
- Too thick before the flavors have melded: add a splash of broth.
- Too thin at the end: add a bit more cornstarch slurry and bring back to a boil.
